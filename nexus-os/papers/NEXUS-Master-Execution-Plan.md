# 1. The strategic position in September 2026

Nexus is entering a market that has already begun moving.

This is important because the strategy cannot be based on the assumption that smart glasses are still an empty frontier.

They are not.

IDC reports that global XR headsets and glasses grew 35.3% year-over-year in Q2 2026. Audio/display-less glasses represented 70.3% of Q2 shipments, while display glasses represented 14.3%. Meta held 68.7% share in IDC's Q2 2026 estimate. IDC forecasts 26.3% unit growth for the full smart-glasses/XR market in 2026, 40.5% in 2027 and 26.4% in 2028.

Meta is simultaneously expanding across audio glasses, display glasses, AI glasses and VR glasses. On September 23–24, 2026 it announced Ray-Ban Meta Audio, Gen 3 improvements, broader styles and new display-glasses functionality; Meta says it expects more than 100 AI-glasses configurations across Ray-Ban, Oakley and Meta Glasses by year-end.

Google is pursuing Android XR as a platform across headsets and intelligent eyewear. Its 2026 plan includes audio and display glasses using Gemini, navigation, environmental understanding, translation, messaging and app integration.

Snap has now moved beyond developer-only Spectacles into SPECS, a standalone see-through AR computer with dual processors, 51° FOV and approximately four hours of mixed-use battery life, with a $2,195 launch price.

The implication is decisive:

> **Nexus cannot spend the next five years proving that glasses can contain cameras, AI, audio, displays or spatial computing.**

Other companies are already proving those things.

Nexus therefore has to prove something deeper:

$$
\boxed{
\text{a new computational architecture}
}
$$

in which the interface is organized around:

$$
\boxed{
\text{human intent}
+
\text{context}
+
\text{world state}
+
\text{continuous interaction}
}
$$

rather than around the traditional:

$$
\text{screen}
+
\text{application}
+
\text{input device}.
$$

# 3. The immediate mission: build the network before building the company

The current objective is **not yet “hire a hundred people.”**

The objective is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{find the people who know how the impossible parts actually work}
}
$$

and build long-term relationships with them.

The network should contain six distinct populations:

| Network                  | Primary purpose                                | When                        |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Current engineers        | Learn how frontier products are actually built | Now                         |
| Ex-engineers             | Recruit people with shipped-product knowledge  | Now                         |
| Researchers/postdocs     | Solve scientifically hard pieces               | Now                         |
| Builder communities      | Rapid experiments and talent discovery         | Now                         |
| Manufacturing/ODM/optics | Convert prototypes into products               | After architecture evidence |
| Investors                | Capitalize proven technical advantage          | Later                       |

Investors should **not** be the first layer.

The first layer should be builders.

# 5. Ex-engineers are especially important

Current employees are valuable for technical perspective but will often have:

* confidentiality restrictions;
* corporate responsibilities;
* limited ability to collaborate externally.

Former engineers can be much more flexible.

One particularly relevant public profile is **Travis Martin**, who describes nine-plus years in HoloLens/Mixed Reality engineering at Microsoft, including drivers, power management, system architecture and foveated rendering. His public profile also describes him as looking for new opportunities after being affected by Microsoft layoffs.
